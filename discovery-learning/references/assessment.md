# Formative assessment and feedback

status: draft

## Evidence
- Unknown: concept presented or learner declined interaction.
- Needs support: observed misconception or incorrect application.
- Recognized: correct constrained choice; weak evidence of independent understanding.
- Applied: correct reasoning or use in a new context.
- Transferred: appropriate application with assumptions, trade-offs or boundaries.

Treat the categories as teaching notes, not numerical grades or validated psychological measures.

## Feedback protocol
Identify the actual misconception, explain the correct rule, show a counterexample, then return to the narrative. Schedule a purposeful callback when the concept is needed again. Do not trap learners in repeated compulsory prompts.

## Quality requirements
Every learning interaction should identify objective ID, facts needed, branching choices, their explanations, relevant misconception, fallback if learner declines, and a natural transfer opportunity. For calculations, verify units, rounding, assumptions and arithmetic separately.

## Safety-sensitive exceptions
If an external training standard requires demonstrated competence or instructor supervision, do not replace the requirement with low-stakes optional choices. Document the exception in the subject course.

## Evaluating the method
Compare comprehension/transfer after a delay, completion, interruption rate, time and learner experience. A more engaging interaction is not necessarily a more effective learning experience.
