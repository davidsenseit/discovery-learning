# Research foundation and limits

status: draft
reviewed_at: not yet independently reviewed

The following are research leads, not a complete systematic review or claims that this exact assembled skill has been empirically proven.

- Dunlosky et al. (2013), *Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques*, Psychological Science in the Public Interest 14(1), 4–58 — comparative review of learning techniques, including practice testing and distributed practice.
- Roediger & Karpicke (2006), *Test-Enhanced Learning*, Psychological Science 17(3), 249–255 — retrieval and later retention.
- Cepeda et al. (2006), *Distributed Practice in Verbal Recall Tasks*, Psychological Bulletin 132(3), 354–380 — meta-analysis of spacing in verbal recall.
- Sweller (1988), *Cognitive Load During Problem Solving*, Cognitive Science 12(2), 257–285 — constraints of working-memory load.
- Hattie & Timperley (2007), *The Power of Feedback*, Review of Educational Research 77(1), 81–112 — feedback processes.
- Wood, Bruner & Ross (1976), *The Role of Tutoring in Problem Solving*, Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry 17(2), 89–100 — scaffolding.
- Rohrer & Taylor (2007), *The Shuffling of Mathematics Problems Improves Learning*, Instructional Science 35, 481–498 — interleaved problem practice in one domain.

## Interpretation
Retrieval practice and distributed practice have broad support, but multiple-choice recognition is not equivalent to unaided retrieval. Spacing only across successive course modules within one sitting is different from practice distributed across days. Interleaving and elaboration depend on material and learner readiness. Narratives and interaction can increase coherence, but may also distract or reduce direct practice; test the trade-off.

## Research integrity
Do not imply that evidence for a component proves the combined method superior. This skill is initially `draft`; no outcome evaluation has been completed.
